Annual Ownership Costs Breakdown

Key recurring expenses associated with owning Japanese property.

🏛️

Fixed Asset Tax

Kotei-shisan-zei is an annual municipal property tax levied at a standard rate of 1.4% on the government-assessed value of land and buildings. Taxes are billed annually in spring.

🗺️

City Planning Tax

Toshi-keikaku-zei is a secondary municipal tax (capped at 0.3%) applicable only to properties located within designated urban planning zones. Many rural mountain properties are exempt.

🛡️

Property Insurance

Comprehensive building insurance covers fire, storm, and snow accumulation damage. Optional Earthquake Insurance (Jishin Hoken) provides government-backed structural protection.

💡

Utilities

Electricity, water/sewerage, LP gas (or kerosene), and fiber internet. Standing monthly basic fees apply even when vacant, with winter heating increasing seasonal usage.

🚜

Snow Clearing

In snow country like Myoko, driveway clearing by heavy machinery contractors and periodic manual roof snow removal are essential winter operating expenses.

🛠️

Routine Maintenance

Preventative building maintenance including roof paint touch-ups, plumbing checks, gutter clearing, heating system servicing, and deck sealing. See our maintenance services.

🏡

Property Management

Boutique management services for non-resident owners covering monthly inspections, bill payments, winterizing, key holding, and guest hosting. Explore our management plans.

Four-Season Property Responsibilities

Understanding year-round care in Myoko’s alpine climate.

❄️ Winter (Dec – Mar)

Heavy Snow & Freeze Prevention

  • Contracting heavy machinery driveway snow clearing.
  • Monitoring roof snow load and clearing overhangs.
  • Maintaining continuous background heating to prevent pipe freezes.
  • Ensuring clear winter access pathways and parking areas.

🌸 Spring (Apr – May)

Post-Winter Inspection & Cleaning

  • Inspecting exterior walls, foundation, and roof for snow damage.
  • Deep interior cleaning and airing out after winter shutdown.
  • Clearing winter debris from gutters, drains, and garden beds.
  • Deactivating heat-trace tapes and adjusting thermostats.

🌿 Summer (Jun – Aug)

Exterior Upkeep & Ventilation

  • Exterior timber staining, painting, and deck maintenance.
  • Mowing lawns, trimming foliage, and garden upkeep.
  • Promoting humidity control and cross-ventilation.
  • Inspecting septic tanks and outdoor drainage systems.

🍁 Autumn (Sep – Nov)

Winterizing & Preparation

  • Servicing Kerosene and gas heating units before cold sets in.
  • Clearing fallen leaves from gutters and drainage channels.
  • Installing snow poles, protective timber shutters, and pipe insulation.
  • Stockpiling Kerosene fuel tanks and testing snowblower equipment.

Frequently Asked Questions About Property Costs

Practical answers based on real experience managing mountain real estate in Myoko.

How much does it cost to own a holiday home in Japan?

Annual ownership costs vary depending on property size, age, location, and frequency of use. Typically, ongoing expenses for a detached holiday home in Myoko include annual property taxes, standing utility charges, winter snow clearing contracts, building insurance, and property management fees. Owners generally budget between ¥300,000 and ¥800,000+ per year for basic operational expenses.

What taxes will I pay each year as a property owner?

Property owners in Japan pay Fixed Asset Tax (Kotei-shisan-zei), which is set at a standard rate of 1.4% of the government-assessed property value. Properties located within designated municipal urban planning zones may also be subject to City Planning Tax (Toshi-keikaku-zei) at 0.3%. Taxes are invoiced annually by the municipal government and can be paid in full or in four quarterly installments.

Do I need specialized insurance for a mountain property in Japan?

Yes, comprehensive building and fire insurance is strongly recommended for mountain properties in Japan. Standard policies should be customized to include specialized coverage for snow accumulation damage, pipe freeze damage, storm impact, and earthquake coverage (Jishin Hoken).

What happens if I live overseas and cannot visit regularly?

For overseas owners, appointing a local property management company is essential. A local manager conducts monthly interior and exterior inspections, handles mail and bill payments, coordinates winter snow clearing, and responds immediately to storm or freeze emergencies.

Who clears the snow from the roof and driveway in Myoko?

Myoko receives extraordinary winter snowfall (13 to 15+ meters annually). Driveways are cleared using contracted heavy machinery operator services (wheel loaders/snowblowers), while roof snow clearing is performed by specialized local snow clearing teams when snow depths exceed safety limits.

Can someone check my property while I am away?

Yes, Love Live Myoko provides regular property inspection services for vacant holiday homes. Our team checks for pipe leaks, roof integrity, ventilation, storm damage, and unauthorized entry, providing photo updates after each visit.

What routine maintenance should I expect for a timber home?

Traditional Japanese timber homes require regular exterior wood staining/painting (every 5-7 years), periodic roof inspections, gutter clearing, plumbing heat-trace tape checks, and deck sealing to prevent moisture rot and winter frost damage.

Can I hire a local property manager in Myoko?

Yes, Love Live Myoko offers complete property management services tailored for non-resident and international owners, including key holding, winterizing, emergency response, and guest check-in support.

What happens if there is an emergency or heavy storm while I am away?

When an emergency, severe typhoon, or heavy snowstorm occurs, a local property manager inspects the site immediately, coordinates emergency repairs with trusted local contractors, and mitigates further damage.

Can I rent the property to short-term guests when I am not using it?

Yes, property owners can operate short-term holiday rentals under Japan’s Minpaku (Private Lodging Business) law or the Hotel Business Act to offset annual ownership costs. Love Live Myoko is a licensed Minpaku Management operator.

Practical Cost Planning Tips

Smart planning ensures your Japanese property remains a rewarding asset rather than a financial burden.

1. Establish an Annual Maintenance Reserve Fund

Set aside 1% to 2% of the property value annually to cover periodic paint touch-ups, roof maintenance, and boiler replacements.

2. Never Skimp on Winterization

Thorough autumn winterization prevents catastrophic pipe bursts and structural water damage during freezing winter months.

3. Maintain Comprehensive Snow Insurance

Ensure your insurance policy explicitly covers snow load structural pressure, ice dam damage, and falling snow impact.
